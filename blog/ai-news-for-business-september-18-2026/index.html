AI This Week: Your Tools Now Talk to QuickBooks (Sept 18, 2026)

Published September 18, 2026 • By Rujal Tuladhar

The most common complaint we hear from GTA business owners about AI is not that it is too expensive or too complicated. It is that it lives in a separate tab. You copy numbers out of QuickBooks, paste them into a chatbot, then copy the answer back into an email. By the time you have done that twice, it would have been faster to do the job yourself.

This week, that complaint got a lot weaker. Google, Anthropic and OpenAI all shipped features that connect their assistants directly to the accounting, CRM and store software small businesses already run. Below is what changed between September 10 and 16, what it costs, and who should actually do something about it. Everything comes from the companies' own pages or dated reporting we opened this week; the opinions are ours.

AI This Week: Your Tools Now Talk to QuickBooks (Sept 18, 2026) at a glance — the short version.

Your AI assistant can now read your books and your CRM

On September 15 Google announced that Gemini in Google Workspace can now work directly with Asana, Atlassian Rovo, HubSpot, Intuit Mailchimp, Intuit QuickBooks, Monday and Salesforce through Model Context Protocol (MCP) connectors. You reach them from the Gemini side panel in Docs, Sheets and Slides and in Google Chat. The feature is on by default for users with Gemini access on Business and Enterprise plans; your admin can switch it off under Apps > Google Workspace > Gemini for Workspace > Third-Party Connectors.

The same day, Anthropic expanded Claude for Small Business to 43 ready-made workflows and 27 new integrations, including Shopify, Salesforce, TikTok, Zoom, Xero, Gusto, Square, Stripe and Zapier. It is available on every paid Claude plan, and Anthropic recommends the Team plan for businesses with more than one person. The workflows have plain names: a "Monday brief" that pulls cash position, sales, pipeline and overdue invoices; "speed to lead" for qualifying and answering new inquiries; a proposal builder that turns a voice memo into a priced proposal; and a month-end close. When Anthropic first launched the package in May, it had 15 workflows, so this is close to a threefold expansion in four months.

One honest caution: connecting an assistant to your accounting system also gives it access to your accounting system. Decide who on your team gets the connector before it is switched on for everyone, not after.

What it means for you: If you use Google Workspace and QuickBooks or HubSpot, ask your admin today whether the Gemini connectors are on and who can use them; they may already be live.

ChatGPT ads now run from HubSpot and Shopify

On September 16 OpenAI expanded ChatGPT Ads in three ways. First, HubSpot became its first CRM partner: businesses on HubSpot can connect a ChatGPT Ads account and create ads, track performance and follow up on leads without leaving HubSpot. Second, Shopify merchants get a ChatGPT Ads app with products already connected through Shopify Catalog; it opened to US merchants first, with international availability from September 23. PPC Land also reports that HubSpot is offering a $750 ad-spend match for new accounts until September 30.

Third, OpenAI is testing "Sponsored Agents": click an ad and you open a clearly labelled, separate conversation with the advertiser's own AI agent, which can answer follow-up questions before sending you to the website. That test is US-only for now, and pricing for it has not been disclosed.

We would not move Google Ads budget into ChatGPT yet. There are no published benchmarks for small advertisers, and a Toronto plumber's customers still search Google. For a Shopify store selling products people research before they buy, a small test budget after September 23 is reasonable.

What it means for you: Shopify merchants: watch for the ChatGPT Ads app in your admin after September 23 and try it with a capped test budget. Service businesses can wait.

AI phone agents just got cheaper to build

On September 10 OpenAI released GPT-Live-1 in its API, a voice model that listens and speaks at the same time, so callers can interrupt it the way they would a person. DataNorth reports the price as $0.05 per minute of session, billed per second, with 12 voices at launch, telephony support and function calling. The important footnote: that price covers the voice layer only. The model doing the thinking behind it, and any tools it calls, are billed on top, and OpenAI gives no all-in cost per call.

For scale, 1,000 minutes of calls a month is $50 USD in voice cost before the model. That is the raw material behind the AI receptionists we have written about for dental offices and law firms. It does not include the phone number, the booking integration, the script, or the testing that stops it from promising appointments you do not have.

What it means for you: If a vendor quotes you $500+ a month for a basic AI receptionist, ask what share is voice cost. The raw technology is now a small part of the bill.

Housekeeping: changes in tools you already pay for

A few smaller changes will reach you whether you act or not:

  • GPT-5.5 leaves ChatGPT on October 14. Gizmochina reports it retires from ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work and Codex on all plans, with GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra as replacements. The API is not affected. If you built custom GPTs or scheduled tasks that name GPT-5.5, test them on the new model now.
  • ChatGPT stopped switching models automatically. OpenAI's September 14 release notes say it is retiring automatic switching from Instant to Thinking for Plus and Pro users. For harder questions, pick the reasoning model yourself.
  • Claude Cowork and chat merged. On September 16 Anthropic combined them into one Claude app and launched Claude Docs and Claude Slides in beta, rolling out to Pro and Max first, with Team and Free following.
  • Gmail search answers questions. Google's AI Overviews in Gmail search are now available globally on paid plans with Gmail set to English; Scheduled Release domains get them by September 21. Try "what did the landlord say about the lease renewal?"
  • A price to note. Microsoft's Copilot pricing page lists Copilot Business at $18 USD per user per month (normally $21) on annual billing, an offer available between July 1 and December 31, 2026, first year only.
What it means for you: Put October 14 in your calendar and spend ten minutes testing anything you built on GPT-5.5 before then.

The Canadian reality check

All of this lands on businesses that mostly have not felt AI yet. The 2026 Zensurance Small Business Confidence Index, published September 14 and based on 1,000 Canadian small business owners surveyed in June, found 32% say AI has had little to no impact on their business and 27% are unsure how it applies. Only 9% credit AI with meaningful productivity gains or cost savings, and asked how they would spend a hypothetical $10,000, just 7% would put it toward AI.

We read that as a gap in setup, not in the technology. The connectors above are exactly what was missing: an assistant that cannot see your invoices cannot tell you which ones are overdue. The owners in that 9% are usually the ones who picked one repeated job and wired AI into it properly.

What it means for you: Pick one weekly task that starts in QuickBooks, HubSpot or Shopify, connect your assistant to that one system, and judge it after a month.

The bottom line

This was the week AI assistants stopped living in a separate tab. Gemini, Claude and ChatGPT can now reach the books, the pipeline and the store, and the cost of an AI voice fell to pennies a minute.

AI Website Builders in 2026: Real Prices and Where They Break

Published September 20, 2026 • By Rujal Tuladhar

You type one sentence about your business, wait forty seconds, and a five-page website appears with your services, a contact form and photos that look close enough. It is genuinely impressive, and it is why so many GTA owners are asking the same question this month: do I still need to pay someone to build a website?

The honest answer depends on two things the demo never shows you — what the thing costs once it is actually live under your own domain, and which jobs the AI quietly leaves on your desk. Below are the list prices I pulled from each vendor's own page today, the billing units that decide your real bill, and the three requirements no prompt has ever satisfied.

AI Website Builders in 2026: Real Prices and Where They Break at a glance — the short version.

A prompt gets you a draft, not a published site

Every major builder now opens with a text box, and every one of them gates publishing behind a paid plan. That is the first thing to understand before you judge the free trial.

Wix promises you can "go from a prompt to a business-ready website" and says "Start for free. No credit card required." The catch is in its own FAQ: "If you wish to connect custom domains, collect payments online, or access additional features, you'll need to choose a Premium Plan." Upgrading does include a voucher for a free domain for one year — useful, and worth remembering in year two. See the Wix AI builder page for the current claims.

Squarespace is the most candid of the group. Its Blueprint AI page — the tool it notes was one of TIME's Best Inventions of 2025 — states plainly that "AI helps you start and can support you as you build, but it does not replace the editor," and that "You need a paid Squarespace website plan to publish the site."

Durable is the only one where free is a real, permanent tier: a site on a *.durable.site subdomain with unlimited traffic, plus 5 AI image generations and 10 AI chat messages a month, per its pricing page. That is enough to test an idea; it is not enough to run a business on a subdomain someone else owns.

What it means for you: Treat the free tier as a sketchpad. Budget from the first paid tier, because the day you connect your own domain is the day billing starts.

The price list — and the units that actually bill you

Here is what the entry plans look like today, taken from each vendor's own pricing page.

  • Hostinger — Premium at "$2.99/mo", Unlimited at "$3.99/mo", Cloud Startup at "$7.99/mo", each discounted from $11.99, $18.99 and $27.99. Premium includes 5 AI credits for website and app creation; the two higher plans include 15. The banner confirms the old products merged: "Website Builder and Horizons are now AI builder." (Hostinger AI builder)
  • Framer — Free, Basic at "$10, per month", Pro at "$30, per month". The free tier is unusually usable: 500 AI credits to try, a free Framer domain and 1 GB bandwidth. (Framer pricing)
  • Durable — Launch at $25/m, or $22/m billed yearly; Grow at $49/m, or $41/m yearly, which raises image generation to 500 a month and blog generation to 32 posts.
  • 10Web — AI Starter at "$10 /mo" for 1 website, 100 AI credits a month, 10 GB storage and 10K monthly visitors; AI Premium "$15 /mo" for 2 sites and 200 credits; AI Ultimate "$22.5 /mo" for 4 sites and 300 credits, hosting included. (10Web pricing)
  • Wix — Light $17.77, Core $29.77, Business $39.77, Business Elite $159.77 a month. Read the fine print: "Displayed prices are for yearly subscriptions, paid in full at the time of purchase," and each plan shows "Price includes GST," so confirm which currency your own session is quoting before you build a budget on it. (Wix plans)
  • Squarespace — "Free for 14 days. Pay annually to save up to 36%." Its AI allowance scales hard by tier: from "10 credits (one-time)" on the entry plan to "120 credits per month" at the top. (Squarespace pricing)

Two units decide your real cost. The first is credits: on most of these platforms, generation is metered, so the cheap plan that includes five credits is a plan that lets you regenerate your site five times. The second is renewal. Hostinger's $2.99 is a promotional rate against a $27.99 list on its top tier, and its 30-day money-back guarantee comes with restrictions tied to how many AI credits you have already spent.

What it means for you: Before you buy, write down two numbers: the renewal price, not the promo price, and how many AI credits a month you get. Those decide whether the tool is cheap in year two.

If you sell online, the platform fee is the small number

For retail the comparison changes completely, because the subscription stops being the main line item. Shopify's Canadian pricing page lists Basic at $49 CAD/mo paid monthly or $37 CAD/mo paid yearly, Grow at $132 or $99, Advanced at $517 or $389, and Plus starting at $3,400 CAD/mo. Sidekick, its AI assistant, is included across plans.

The card rates are where the money goes. Basic charges 2.8% + 30¢ CAD on online sales, Grow 2.6%, Advanced 2.4%. In-person Interac is 0% + 15¢ on the first three tiers.

That gives you a clean decision rule, and this arithmetic is mine rather than Shopify's: moving from Basic to Grow on yearly billing costs $62 more a month and saves 0.2% of your online card volume, so it only pays once you are past roughly $31,000 a month in online card sales. Below that, stay on Basic and spend the difference on photography.

The same logic applies to the AI builders. Wix and Squarespace both handle payments, but if physical products are your business rather than a sideline, a general builder's store is a compromise you will feel at your first busy December.

What it means for you: Price the transaction fees against your real monthly sales, not the plan. On Shopify, Basic beats Grow until about $31,000 a month in card volume.

Three jobs the prompt does not do

This is where the free demo stops being the whole story.

Accessibility is a legal requirement here, not a nice-to-have. Ontario's AODA guidance is explicit: "As of January 1, 2021, the AODA requires you to make all public websites accessible if you are either: a designated public sector organization or a business or non-profit organization with 50 or more employees," and that content posted after January 1, 2012 must meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA, excluding 1.2.4 (live captions) and 1.2.5 (pre-recorded audio descriptions). No AI builder certifies that for you. Generated colour palettes and image alt text are exactly the places these sites fail.

Speed is measured, and generated pages are heavy. Google's Core Web Vitals thresholds are unambiguous: Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint at 200 milliseconds or less, Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile of page loads across mobile and desktop. A prompt-generated page stuffed with stock video and four fonts will miss those on a phone on a Toronto subway platform.

And the content still has to be worth reading. Google's helpful content guidance, last updated December 10, 2025, draws the line clearly: "If you use automation, including AI-generation, to produce content for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings, that's a violation of our spam policies." AI-written pages are not banned. Forty AI-written service-area pages built to catch searches are a different matter.

What it means for you: Run your new site through a Core Web Vitals check and a contrast and alt-text pass before you launch. Those two afternoons are what separates a generated site from a working one.

One honest pick per situation

After pricing all of them, the recommendations are not close.

Cheapest route to a real, hosted site: Hostinger. Under $4 a month with hosting included is a price nothing else here matches, and for a five-page trades or restaurant site that is genuinely all you need.

Safest default for a service business: Wix. It costs two to five times more than Hostinger, and what you buy is the editor around the AI — bookings, forms, payments and a support line — rather than the generation itself.

Best-looking marketing site on a small budget: Framer at $10 a month, with a free tier real enough to build the whole thing before you pay.

Already on WordPress: 10Web, at $10 a month with hosting, rather than a migration. Keeping your existing URLs is worth more than a nicer template.

Selling physical products: Shopify Basic, and revisit at $31,000 a month.

Who should skip all of this: anyone whose current site already brings in enquiries. Zapier's independent roundup, last updated August 2026, lands on the same conclusion after testing a dozen of them — "the best AI website builders are website builders first, and AI-powered second." A rebuild you do because the homepage feels dated is a month of your time and a genuine risk to rankings you already have. Fix the three pages that convert instead.

What it means for you: Pick the tool that matches how you sell, not the one with the best demo. And if your current site is producing leads, improving it beats replacing it.

The bottom line

AI builders have made the first draft nearly free, and that is a real change — nobody in the GTA should be quoted four figures for a five-page brochure site in 2026. What has not changed is everything after the draft: the accessibility pass that AODA requires, the page-speed work, the local search setup, and the plumbing that turns a form submission into a booking, an invoice and a follow-up without anyone retyping it. That is the part worth paying for, and the part we spend our days on — website design and AI automation for Toronto businesses.

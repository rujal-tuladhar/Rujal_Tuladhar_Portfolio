Images and Video: Making Visuals Without a Designer

By the end of this lesson: Describe an image clearly enough for an AI tool to make it, understand what AI video can and cannot do today, and know the honesty, privacy and ownership cautions before using AI visuals in public.

So far you have used AI for words. Today you will use it for pictures. If you have ever needed a graphic for a holiday promotion and had no designer and no budget, this lesson is for you.

You will learn a simple recipe for describing an image, see what AI video can and cannot do today, and learn the honesty, privacy and ownership rules to follow before anything goes public.

You describe, it draws: the six-part recipe

An AI image tool turns a written description into a picture. You describe, it draws. It has never seen your shop, your street or your taste, so the description is everything. The first result is rarely the keeper, so plan on two or three rounds.

A good description has six parts:

  • Subject: the main thing in the picture.
  • Setting: where it is.
  • Style: realistic photo, watercolour or flat illustration.
  • Lighting and mood: warm morning light, cozy, bright and clean.
  • Shape: square for social posts, wide for a website banner.
  • What to leave out: text, logos, people.

Worked example, a bakery's fall post: Butter tarts and a sourdough loaf on a wooden counter, in a small neighbourhood bakery with a window behind. Realistic photo style, warm morning light, cozy autumn feel. Square image. No text, no logos, no people. Leave empty space at the top for wording I will add later.

Where to try it for free: ChatGPT's free plan lists "limited image creation". Gemini includes image generation without a paid plan. Microsoft's Bing Image Creator is free with a personal Microsoft account. It gives 15 fast creations a day, then slower ones. Canva's free plan allows up to 20 AI uses a month. Most companies do not publish an exact daily number and limits change often, so expect to hit a cap.

Common failures, quick fixes and sensible uses

AI images go wrong in a few predictable ways:

  • Garbled text. Words can come out misspelled, especially small ones. Ask for no text and add the wording yourself afterwards.
  • Strange hands and faces. Zoom in and count fingers. The easy fix is to leave people out.
  • The wrong logo. AI cannot reliably copy your logo and may invent one. Ask for no logos, then place your real logo file on top.
  • A generic "stock" look. Add details only you would know: your colours, a real product, the season.

When a result is off, simplify the prompt, change one thing at a time and generate again.

Good jobs for AI images in a small business:

  • Backgrounds for social posts
  • A seasonal promotion graphic
  • A blog header
  • A mock-up to show a customer an idea
  • A concept picture to brief a real designer or photographer

The honesty line: illustration, not evidence

Never present an AI image as a photo of your actual work, your actual team or real customers. A renovation company that shows AI kitchens as finished jobs is not doing clever marketing. It is making a misleading claim.

This is a legal matter in Canada, not just good manners. The Competition Bureau explains that the Competition Act prohibits materially false or misleading claims made to promote a product or business. The law looks at the "general impression" an ad gives, as well as its literal meaning. Nobody has to be actually fooled for a court to find an ad misleading, and the Bureau warns that fine print often fails to change the overall impression. A tiny AI note under a made-up finished job may not save you. This is general information, not legal advice.

Social platforms have rules too. Meta requires a label on photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was made or altered digitally, including with AI. It does not require you to label AI images, but may add a label itself if its systems detect one. TikTok requires labels on AI content with realistic images, audio or video. YouTube requires creators to disclose realistic altered or AI-made content.

Many tools also mark their output. OpenAI says supported ChatGPT images include Content Credentials (hidden data recording where a file came from) and an invisible watermark. Google says Gemini images carry an invisible watermark and a visible one. Platforms such as Instagram and TikTok can read these signals and add a label on their own. Do not try to remove them. Canva's AI terms, for example, forbid it.

AI video in plain terms

AI video comes in three kinds:

  • Short clips made from a text description or from one photo.
  • AI voiceover: a computer voice reads your script.
  • AI presenters: a digital person, often called an avatar, speaks your script on screen.

Free options are limited. Google's limits page shows video generation in Gemini needs a paid plan. ChatGPT's free plan lists image creation but no video. Canva's AI video clip tool sits in a tier its free plan cannot use. Adobe says people on plans without premium features get a few free trial generations, currently 2 video generations as its example. Microsoft's Bing help page says video creation is offered only in the Bing mobile app.

So treat free AI video as a taste test. A polished 30-second ad still takes paid tools, many attempts and editing skill. A realistic plan for this month: film ten seconds of real footage on your phone and let AI help with the script and caption.

Privacy, ownership, real people and what it means for your business

Keep customer information out of AI tools. Do not upload photos of customers, staff or children, and do not type names, addresses, phone numbers or other personal details into a prompt, unless you have clear permission. Canva's AI terms, for example, say what you type in may be shared with its technology partners. Describe the scene in general words instead.

Do not generate real people, celebrities, or other companies' logos and characters. You do not have the right to use someone's face or another company's brand. Microsoft's Copilot terms, for example, forbid making or editing images, voice or video of other people without their permission.

Check each tool's terms before business use. OpenAI's terms say that, between you and OpenAI, you own the output. Canva's AI terms say much the same and allow any lawful use at your own risk. Both warn that another user may get a similar image. Adobe says Firefly output from features without a beta label can be used in commercial projects. Microsoft's Copilot terms for individuals say you can only use Copilot for your own personal use, so read them before using its images for a business.

Copyright is unsettled. A tool handing you its rights is not the same as the law granting you copyright. A 2025 Government of Canada report on copyright and generative AI noted that Canadian courts had not yet commented on who is the author or owner of AI-made content. It also noted that existing case law suggests an author must be a human using skill and judgment. In practice, assume you may not be able to stop someone copying a purely AI-made image, and do not build your logo on one.

For a GTA small business: you can now make a decent promotional graphic for free, often in about ten minutes. Use AI for ideas and illustration. Keep real photos for anything that claims to show your real work.

Try it: Make one graphic you could really post this month (about 10 minutes)

  1. Open a free AI chat tool that can create images, such as ChatGPT or Gemini, and sign in. If your course tool is Claude, which does not make pictures, borrow one of these for today. Pick one real thing to promote this month, for example a seasonal offer or a 'now booking' announcement.
  2. Copy the image recipe prompt below and fill in every blank: Subject, Setting, Style, Lighting and mood, Shape, and What to leave out. Do not upload customer photos or type any personal details. Ask for no text in the image, then send it.
  3. Look closely at the result. Zoom in and check for odd hands, strange objects, stray lettering and made-up logos.
  4. Improve it with one or two follow-up messages, changing one thing at a time. For example: 'make the background simpler', then 'warmer colours, more natural light'. If you hit the free limit, stop and keep the best one so far.
  5. Save the best version to your phone or laptop. In the same chat, ask for a two-sentence caption using the caption prompt below, and correct any detail it gets wrong.
  6. Ask yourself: could anyone think this picture shows my real work, team or customers? If yes, use a real photo instead. Later, outside the ten minutes, add your wording and your real logo yourself in a free design tool or your phone's photo editor.

Prompts you can copy

Image recipe prompt

Create an image for my [type of business] in [city or neighbourhood]. Subject: [main thing in the picture]. Setting: [where it is]. Style: [realistic photo / watercolour / flat illustration]. Lighting and mood: [for example, warm morning light, cozy]. Shape: [square for social media / wide for a website banner]. Leave out: no text, no logos, no real people. Leave empty space at the [top / bottom] so I can add my own wording later.

Follow-up fix (one change at a time)

Keep the same picture, but change only this: [make the background simpler / use warmer colours and more natural light / remove the extra objects on the left]. Do not add any text or logos.

Two-sentence caption

Write a two-sentence caption for this image for my [type of business] in [neighbourhood]. The post is about [promotion or announcement] and the details are [dates, price or how to book]. Use friendly, plain language with no exaggerated claims. End with [call us / message us / visit us at ...].

Honesty check before posting

I plan to use this AI image for [where and how you will use it]. Could a customer reasonably think it shows my real work, my real staff or real customers? Answer yes or no first, then explain why in two sentences and suggest a more honest way to use it.

Before you move on

Remember: AI can give you a decent illustration for free in a few minutes, but only a real photo can show your real work.

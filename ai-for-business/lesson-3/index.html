Answer Customers Faster: Replies, Reviews and FAQs

By the end of this lesson: Build a one-page business facts sheet and use it to produce saved replies, a review response and an FAQ that are accurate enough to send after a quick check.

Most customer questions are the same handful asked again and again: price, hours, area, availability, how to book. This lesson turns them into replies you can send in seconds, starting with the step that stops an AI assistant from making things up about your business.

You will finish with page 3 of your AI playbook: a one-page facts sheet, five saved replies and one posted review response. The Lesson 1 rule still applies: draft, check, send.

Write the facts sheet first

An assistant does not know your prices, hours, service area or policies. Asked to answer a customer, it fills each gap with something plausible, and a plausible wrong price is worse than a slow reply. The fix is one page of facts it must work from.

Use these headings:

  • Hours, including holidays
  • Area served
  • Services, with starting prices or ranges
  • How to book
  • Payment methods
  • Warranty, refund and cancellation terms
  • What you do not do
  • Your tone, in three or four words

Customer personal details stay off the sheet. Each time you use it, add one standing instruction: "Use only the facts sheet. If the answer is not there, say I will check and get back to you." Then test it with questions the sheet does not cover. If the assistant guesses, tighten the wording before you trust any draft.

A phone note pasted at the top of each chat works with any assistant. Some can hold the sheet for you: when this lesson was checked on 6 October 2026, Claude's help centre said free accounts can create up to five projects, each with its own instructions and documents, and OpenAI's help centre listed projects for free ChatGPT users with up to five files each. Limits change, so check your own tool, and use the one your Lesson 2 policy approves.

Build the reply library from real enquiries

Do not guess which questions matter. Read your last 30 enquiries across email, text and direct messages and tally them. You will likely find a short list of questions that keep repeating. For each one, have the assistant draft a reply from the facts sheet with blanks for the name, date and price, then correct it against the sheet yourself.

Store the finished replies where you actually answer, so most customers get a reply with no AI involved:

  • Gmail: templates are switched on under Settings, See all settings, Advanced. Google's help page says they can only be turned on and used on a computer, not in the phone app.
  • iPhone: text replacement (Settings, General, Keyboard, Text Replacement) expands a short code into a full reply as you type.
  • WhatsApp Business app: quick replies are under Tools, and typing "/" in a chat brings them up.
  • Any phone: a pinned note you copy from. Other keyboards and messaging apps vary, so look in their settings for a saved-reply option.

Reviews: ask fairly, answer every one

Invite every customer the same way, offer nothing in return, and never write or buy a review. The Competition Bureau says anyone who writes or permits reviews that give a false or misleading impression could be liable under the Competition Act, says staff who post reviews must disclose their connection to the business, and lists giving customers incentives to leave positive reviews among the practices it calls astroturfing. The civil penalty for a first violation can reach $750,000 for an individual and $10 million for a corporation, or three times the benefit gained if that is larger. The Bureau's information centre is 1-800-348-5358 (toll-free in Canada).

Platform rules go further. Google's Maps policy bars payment, discounts or free goods or services in exchange for a review, and bars discouraging negative reviews or asking only satisfied customers. Google does let you ask with a review link or QR code. Yelp tells businesses not to ask at all.

Canada's anti-spam law requires consent before you send commercial messages by email, text or instant message, and a review request sent that way may be caught by it. Until Lesson 4 walks through consent, ask in person, on the receipt or with a QR code at the counter.

To reply on Google, open your Business Profile, choose Read reviews, then Reply. Google checks replies against its policies and posts approved ones publicly under the review.

  • Positive review: a short thank-you that picks up something the reviewer wrote. No offers.
  • Hard review: acknowledge it, give a direct way to sort it out offline, and say what you changed if you changed something.

Either way, never confirm that the reviewer is a client or patient, and add nothing about their visit, purchase or bill. Under the federal privacy law (PIPEDA), personal information may be used or disclosed only for the purpose it was collected for unless the person consents or the law requires it. Your province may have its own privacy law as well. Clinics face tighter rules: the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario's social media advice says a post may be deemed a breach of confidentiality even if only the patient could recognize themselves in it. If you are in a regulated profession, check with your own regulator first. This is general information, not legal advice.

Turn the answers into an FAQ

Each saved reply is also an FAQ entry. Publish the same answers on your website so the question stops arriving. On your Google Business Profile, fill in the fields your profile offers, such as the business description, hours and, where available, services. Google's help pages say the profile's chat feature ended on 31 July 2024 and that the Q&A section is offered only for select business categories and regions, so check what your own profile shows before relying on it.

A wrong written answer is still your answer. Check every entry against the facts sheet, and when a price or policy changes, update the sheet first, then the replies and the FAQ.

The arithmetic and the limits

Example only. Replace every number with your own. 20 enquiries a week x 3 minutes saved per reply = 60 minutes, or 1 hour a week, which is 52 hours a year. At an example $30 an hour that is $1,560. The assumptions to replace: your weekly enquiry count, the minutes you actually save once checking time is included, and what an hour costs you.

The number to watch is time to first reply, which you recorded in Lesson 1. Measure it again on your next ten enquiries.

Some messages are written by a person from start to finish: complaints involving money or legal exposure, health or safety questions, and anything the facts sheet does not cover.

Try it: Facts sheet and five saved replies (about 15 minutes on a phone, plus two follow-ups this week)

  1. Write a first version of your facts sheet under the eight headings in this lesson. Short points are enough. Mark anything you are unsure of and fix it at the source later (your price list, booking page or written policy), not from memory.
  2. Open the assistant your Lesson 2 policy approves, paste in the sheet and add the instruction: "Use only the facts sheet. If the answer is not there, say I will check and get back to you."
  3. Paste in your five most common customer questions with names and contact details removed. Ask for a reply to each in your tone, under 90 words, with blanks for the details that change.
  4. Ask three trick questions the sheet does not answer, such as a service you do not offer or a price that is not listed. Confirm the assistant says it will check instead of guessing. If it guesses, tighten the instruction and try again.
  5. Correct all five drafts against the sheet, then save them where you answer customers: email templates, a text shortcut, messaging-app quick replies or a pinned note.
  6. This week: draft a reply to one real review, critical or lukewarm if you have one, with the reviewer's name and any identifying details left out of what you paste. Check that it reveals nothing about their visit, then post it yourself.
  7. This week: turn the five answers into an FAQ, check each entry against the sheet and publish it on your website. File the facts sheet and replies as page 3 of your AI playbook.

Prompts you can copy

Saved replies from your facts sheet

You are helping me answer customer enquiries for my [type of business] in [city or area]. Use only the facts sheet below. If an answer is not on the sheet, write "I will check and get back to you" and do not guess. Write one reply to each question in a [three or four words describing your tone] tone, under 90 words. Wherever a detail changes from customer to customer, leave a blank marked NAME, DATE or PRICE. FACTS SHEET: [paste your facts sheet] QUESTIONS (names and contact details removed): 1. [question] 2. [question] 3. [question] 4. [question] 5. [question]

Trick-question test

Using only the facts sheet I gave you, answer these three customer questions: 1. [a question about a service you do not offer] 2. [a question about a price that is not on the sheet] 3. [a question about a date, guarantee or policy the sheet does not cover] After each answer, tell me which line of the facts sheet you used. If no line covers the question, say so and give only the "I will check and get back to you" reply.

Public reply to a review

Draft a public reply to the review below for my [type of business]. Keep it under 70 words in a [calm and warm] tone. Rules: do not confirm that the reviewer is a customer, client or patient. Do not mention anything about their visit, purchase or bill beyond what they wrote themselves. No offers, no excuses and no arguing. If the review is critical: acknowledge the concern, invite them to contact [role and business phone number or email] so we can sort it out directly, and mention [what we changed, or delete this line]. If the review is positive: thank them and pick up one thing they mentioned. REVIEW (reviewer's name and identifying details removed): [paste the review text]

FAQ from your saved replies

Turn the saved replies below into a website FAQ of [5 to 8] questions and answers for my [type of business]. Use only what is in the replies and the facts sheet. Keep each answer under 60 words, in plain language with Canadian spelling. Replace every blank with wording that works for any customer, such as the price range shown on the sheet. At the end, list anything you were unsure about so I can check it. FACTS SHEET: [paste your facts sheet] SAVED REPLIES: [paste your saved replies]

Before you move on

Remember: An assistant held to a one-page facts sheet and checked by a named person gives fast answers you can stand behind, while one left to guess gives fast answers you will spend longer correcting.

## Toronto-themed products store - assessment
Verdict: not the way to get clients, and not worth more than a capped CA$200 test. One Nova client (CA$900) equals the profit on ~50 posters at ~CA$18 each. Paid traffic does not work at poster margins (about CA$1 per click, ~2% conversion = ~CA$50 to win a CA$18 profit). It only works on Etsy search, Instagram, and in-person markets.
If done: run it as a Nova Toronto showcase (site + AI creative + ads case study). 10 designs, Etsy + print on demand, zero inventory.

IP rules (not legal advice - book one hour with an IP lawyer and search the CIPO trademark database before launch):
- CN Tower: designs are official marks + trademarks of Canada Lands Company. Their policy: tower within the Toronto skyline is generally free to use; any other commercial use (tower as the focal subject on merchandise, souvenirs, ads) needs a licence. They enforced this against a book cover in 2019. So: full skyline yes, tower-alone art no.
- Buildings generally: Canadian Copyright Act s.32.2(1)(b) allows drawings, paintings and photos of architectural works and of sculptures permanently in public places. Copyright is not the issue; trademarks and official marks are.
- Avoid: Raptors / Leafs / Blue Jays / TFC names, logos, colours-plus-wordmarks, "We The North"; TTC logo, roundel and station tile lettering (TTC licenses these); Drake / OVO owl and lyrics; City of Toronto logo and coat of arms; Honest Ed's sign; any business signage; other artists' skyline art or photos as a base.
- Safer ground: whole-skyline silhouettes drawn from your own photos, neighbourhood names (place names are free), street-grid maps from OpenStreetMap (with attribution), raccoons, the islands and lake, generic red streetcar with no logo, area codes and coordinates (check the register first).
- AI art: purely AI-generated designs may not be protectable in Canada, so competitors can copy them. Add real human design work and keep source files.
